Mosquitoes

Why mosquitoes bite more at dusk (and what to do about it)

Dr. Aisha Bello Sep 24, 20266 min read

If you’ve ever sat outside at sunset and suddenly been swarmed, it isn’t your imagination. Several of the most common backyard mosquitoes are crepuscular — most active in the hours around dawn and dusk.

Why dusk?

Mosquitoes lose water fast. Midday sun and heat dry them out, so they rest in shady, humid foliage. As temperatures drop and humidity rises in the evening, they leave those resting spots to look for a meal. Still air at dusk also makes it easier for them to follow the carbon dioxide you breathe out.

Treat the resting spots, and you stop the evening swarm before it starts.

Three ways to reclaim your evenings

  1. Remove standing water weekly. Eggs can hatch into biting adults in about a week.
  2. Use a fan on the patio. Mosquitoes are weak fliers; a gentle breeze helps more than you’d think.
  3. Treat the foliage, not the air. A barrier treatment on shrubs and shady areas lasts for weeks, where foggers last for hours.

The bottom line

Dusk biting is predictable — which makes it preventable. Keep water out of your yard and keep a fresh barrier on the places mosquitoes rest, and evenings outside get a lot more pleasant.
